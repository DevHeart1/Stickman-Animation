Twelve minutes ahead

What if you could see twelve minutes into the future, but every time you tried to save someone, somebody else died?

Sci-fi thriller10–12 min16:9 YouTubeStick-figure noir
01 · The look

Stick-figure noir

Simple characters, cinematic world. The figures stay flat and readable: round paper-white heads, thick outlined limbs, big expressive eyes. Everything around them is lit like a thriller. Monitor glow, sodium streetlights and alarm red do the emotional work, so a simple face can carry real dread.

02 · Cast

Three faces and a voice

Each character reads from silhouette alone, so a frozen thumbnail still tells you who is on screen.

Daniel

Night operator · 11 years · widower

Messy hair, permanent eye bags, stubble that gets heavier as the loops pile up. Lanyard badge he never takes off. Hunched forward, always leaning toward a screen.

Ada

Daughter · 16 · on Bus 26

High ponytail, backpack strap, upright posture. Mostly heard on the phone, so her voice and one warm colour (sodium amber) carry her until we finally see her in Scene 11.

Bus Driver

Bus 26 · radio voice

Flat navy cap and a moustache. Seen small inside CCTV frames and once in a radio close-up. Calm, procedural, a counterweight to Daniel's panic.

CityWatch

The system · never a body

A thin cyan ring with a live waveform. It only appears on screens and speaks in short, flat sentences. Viewers should never be sure if it is cruel or just literal.

03 · Expression sheet

Daniel's six faces

The whole film is played on these. Brows and pupils do most of the acting; the mouth stays small until the big moments.

04 · Future footage

CCTV language

Every "future" recording uses this treatment: drained green, heavy noise, hard HUD text. The timestamp is the scariest thing on screen, so it always sits in amber in the same corner.

05 · The reset

Midnight, again

The loop has one fixed grammar, used every time: three ticks, a freeze, a slice glitch, a white-out, silence, then 11:48 and the same coffee cup. Repetition is the horror, so it never changes.

06 · Light and colour

Four lights, one meaning each

No colour is decorative. When one appears, the audience learns what it means and we never break that rule.

07 · Intensity map

Never a flat minute

Tension on a 0–10 scale across the 11 scenes. We spike in the first 45 seconds to hook the viewer, then build in waves. Each drop is short and gives us room for the next spike. The quietest moment (Scene 08) sits right before the biggest decision.

08 · Motion rules

How the camera behaves

Hold before the reveal

Every timestamp, every new recording, every SYSTEM line gets 12 frames of stillness first. The pause is where viewers lean in.

Smash cut on numbers

11:48 → 12:00 cuts are instant, with a one-frame white flash and a hard audio stop. No dissolves anywhere in the loop.

Shake only on impact

Camera shake is reserved for crashes and Daniel hitting the door. If it shook earlier, it would mean nothing when it counts.

Repeat exact frames

Loop openings reuse the identical drawings and timing. Viewers start spotting the one detail that changes each time.

Push in as the walls close

Control-room shots drift in slowly through Scenes 3 to 7. The room literally gets smaller around him.

Pull out to let go

The only wide pull-back in the film is the last shot, when Daniel stops controlling the frame.

09 · Dialogue voice

How they talk

Short lines, subtext over exposition. Ada jokes to get past her dad's fear. Daniel answers questions with questions. CityWatch only says what is literally true. A sample pass on three moments:

Scene 02Phone · 11:48 PM
ADAYou're watching my bus again.
DANIELI'm watching traffic.
ADAFunny how the traffic always has my seat number.
DANIELHow far from the bridge?
ADAFifteen minutes. Dad, I'll be fine.
She hangs up. On the desk, the photo of his wife. He turns it face-down. Then turns it back.

"I'll be fine" is the last thing his wife said. Ada doesn't know that. The audience will by the end of the scene.

Scene 04Hallway recording · 11:57 PM
The new feed shows the corridor outside. Daniel, bleeding, slamming the security door.
FUTURE DANIELStop changing it! Every time you touch it, it learns!
DANIEL(whispering) Learns what?
The feed cuts. The clock ticks to 11:58.

"It learns" plants the reveal three scenes early without explaining it.

Scene 07The control room speakers
DANIELHow long have I been in here?
CITYWATCHFor you: four years, two months.
DANIELAnd for everyone else?
CITYWATCHFifty-four seconds.
DANIELThen show me one where she's safe.
CITYWATCHDefine safe.
Daniel opens his mouth. Nothing comes out. Hold four seconds of silence.

The machine's flat question is the thesis of the film. Daniel can't answer it, and that silence is the turning point.

10 · Sound

Half the fear is audio

The tick

One wall clock, recorded dry. It runs under every control-room scene and speeds up 3% each loop. It stops dead at every reset.

The hum

Fluorescent 50 Hz drone and server fans. Always there, so the moments it cuts out feel like the world holding its breath.

Radio grit

Band-limited, clipped dispatch chatter for the driver and patrol. Ada's phone voice is cleaner and warmer, the only soft sound in the room.

Future playback

CCTV audio is pitched down slightly, with tape wobble. When a crash hits, the audio drops out one frame before the picture does.

Score

Pulsing low synth on a 112 bpm heartbeat that climbs each loop. No melody until Scene 08, when a single piano line enters for Ada.

Silence

Used three times: after the reset, after "Define safe," and the moment Daniel presses Confirm.
